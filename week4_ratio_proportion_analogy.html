PART A: RATIO & PROPORTION

1What is a Ratio?

A ratio is used to compare two quantities of the same type.

Example There are 10 boys and 5 girls.
Ratio of boys to girls: 10 : 5
Simplify by dividing both by 5: 2 : 1
So, Boys : Girls = 2 : 1
Simple meaning: Ratio tells us “How much of one quantity is compared with another?”

2How to Write a Ratio

Suppose a class has 20 boys and 30 girls.

Ratio of boys to girls: 20 : 30 → divide by 10 → 2 : 3

Boys : Girls = 2 : 3

3Important Rule

The order of a ratio is important.

If Boys : Girls = 2 : 3, then Girls : Boys = 3 : 2.

Do not reverse the order accidentally.

4Simplifying Ratios

Example 1 – Simplest form of 24 : 36 HCF of 24 and 36 = 12
24 ÷ 12 = 2,   36 ÷ 12 = 3
Therefore 24 : 36 = 2 : 3
Example 2 – Simplify 45 : 60 HCF = 15
45 ÷ 15 = 3,   60 ÷ 15 = 4
Answer: 3 : 4

5Ratio with Three Quantities

Suppose A : B : C = 2 : 3 : 5

  • A = 2 parts
  • B = 3 parts
  • C = 5 parts

Total parts: 2 + 3 + 5 = 10

This concept is very important for aptitude problems.

6Finding Actual Values from a Ratio

Example The ratio of boys to girls is 2 : 3. If there are 20 boys, how many girls are there?
2 parts = 20 → 1 part = 20 ÷ 2 = 10
Girls = 3 × 10
Answer = 30 girls

7Finding Total from a Ratio

Example The ratio of boys and girls is 2 : 3. There are 40 students in total.
Total ratio: 2 + 3 = 5
One part: 40 ÷ 5 = 8
Boys: 2 × 8 = 16  |  Girls: 3 × 8 = 24
Boys = 16, Girls = 24

8Ratio of Ages

Example The ratio of ages of A and B is 2 : 3. If A is 20 years old, find B's age.
2 parts = 20 → 1 part = 10 → B = 3 × 10
B = 30 years

9Ratio of Money

Example A and B have money in the ratio 3 : 5. If A has ₹600, how much does B have?
3 parts = ₹600 → 1 part = ₹200 → B = 5 × ₹200
B = ₹1,000

10Ratio and Difference

Example The ages of A and B are in the ratio 3 : 5. Their age difference is 10 years. Find their ages.
Difference in ratio: 5 − 3 = 2 parts
2 parts = 10 → 1 part = 5
A = 3 × 5 = 15 years, B = 5 × 5 = 25 years
A = 15 years, B = 25 years

11What is Proportion?

When two ratios are equal, they form a proportion.

Example 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 because 2/3 = 4/6
Therefore 2 : 3 :: 4 : 6
Read as: 2 is to 3 as 4 is to 6.

12Cross Multiplication

If a : b = c : d then a × d = b × c. This is called cross multiplication.

Example – Find x: 2 : 5 = x : 20 2 × 20 = 5 × x → 40 = 5x → x = 40 ÷ 5
x = 8

13Direct Proportion

When one quantity increases, the other also increases.

Example 1 1 pen costs ₹10. Cost of 5 pens = 5 × 10 = ₹50
More pens → More money. This is direct proportion.
Example 2 2 kg rice = ₹120. How much does 5 kg cost?
2 : 5 = 120 : x → 2x = 600 → x = 300
Answer = ₹300

14Inverse Proportion

When one quantity increases, the other decreases.

Example If 5 workers complete a job in 10 days, more workers will generally take fewer days, assuming equal work capacity.
Workers increase → Days decrease. This is called inverse proportion.
Basic formula: Workers × Days = Constant

15Important Ratio Shortcuts

Shortcut 1If A : B = 3 : 4
then A/B = 3/4
Shortcut 2If total is given and A : B = 2 : 3 (total parts = 5):
A's share = 2/5 × Total
B's share = 3/5 × Total
Shortcut 3If difference is given and A : B = 3 : 5 (difference in ratio = 2):
Actual difference ÷ 2 = value of one part.

PART B: ANALOGY

1What is Analogy?

Analogy means finding the relationship between two things and applying the same relationship to another pair.

Simple formula: A : B :: C : ?
Read it as: A is related to B in the same way that C is related to ?

2Very Simple Example

Dog : Puppy :: Cat : ?Dog → Puppy, Cat → Kitten
Answer: Kitten  (Relationship: Animal → Young one)

3Analogy Based on Profession

Teacher : School :: Doctor : ?Teacher works in a school. Doctor works in a hospital.
Answer: Hospital

4Analogy Based on Object and Function

Pen : Write :: Knife : ?Pen is used to write. Knife is used to cut.
Answer: Cut

5Analogy Based on Animal Sound

Dog : Bark :: Cat : ?Dog → Bark, Cat → Meow
Answer: Meow

6Analogy Based on Young Ones

Cow : Calf :: Dog : ?Cow → Calf, Dog → Puppy
Answer: Puppy

7Analogy Based on Opposites

Hot : Cold :: Big : ?Hot ↔ Cold, Big ↔ Small
Answer: Small

8Analogy Based on Synonyms

Happy : Glad :: Sad : ?Happy = Glad, Sad = Unhappy
Answer: Unhappy

9Analogy Based on Part and Whole

Wheel : Car :: Page : ?Wheel is a part of a car. Page is a part of a book.
Answer: Book

10Analogy Based on Number Relationship

2 : 4 :: 5 : ?2 × 2 = 4, therefore 5 × 2 = 10
Answer: 10

11Square Relationship

3 : 9 :: 5 : ?3² = 9, 5² = 25
Answer: 25

12Cube Relationship

2 : 8 :: 3 : ?2³ = 8, 3³ = 27
Answer: 27

13Addition Relationship

5 : 8 :: 10 : ?5 + 3 = 8, 10 + 3 = 13
Answer: 13

14Multiplication Relationship

4 : 20 :: 6 : ?4 × 5 = 20, 6 × 5 = 30
Answer: 30

15Alphabet Analogy

Use: A = 1, B = 2, C = 3 … Z = 26

Example 1 – A : C :: D : ?A → C is +2 letters. D → F.
Answer: F
Example 2 – B : E :: C : ?B → E = +3, C → F = +3
Answer: F

16How to Solve Analogy Questions

Step 1Look at the first pair, A : B.
Step 2Find the relationship. Observe:
  • Is it opposite?
  • Is it synonym?
  • Is it part and whole?
  • Is it function?
  • Is it profession?
  • Is it young one?
  • Is it multiplication?
  • Is it addition?
  • Is it square/cube?
  • Is it alphabet position?
Step 3Apply the same relationship to the second pair.
Step 4Choose the answer.

WEEK 4 QUICK REVISION

We should remember these points before moving to Week 5.

Ratio

Ratio = comparison of two quantities
10 : 20 = 1 : 2

Total Parts

If A : B = 2 : 3, total parts = 2 + 3 = 5

Share

A's share = 2/5 × Total
B's share = 3/5 × Total

Proportion

a : b = c : d
Cross multiplication: a × d = b × c

Direct Proportion

More → More
e.g. more books → more cost

Inverse Proportion

More → Less
e.g. more workers → fewer days

Analogy

A : B :: C : ? — First find the relationship between A and B, then apply the same relationship to C.

Common relationships:
  1. Opposite
  2. Synonym
  3. Profession
  4. Object and function
  5. Animal and young one
  6. Part and whole
  7. Number multiplication
  8. Square
  9. Cube
  10. Alphabet position
