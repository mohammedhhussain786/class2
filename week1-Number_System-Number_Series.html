Part B — Aptitude

Number System

What is a number? A mathematical value used to represent quantity — for example 1, 2, 3, 10, 100, 500. Numbers are classified into several types, shown below.

Natural numbers

Counting numbers starting from 1. Do not include 0.

1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …

Whole numbers

Natural numbers including zero.

0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …

Integers

Positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.

…, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …

Even numbers

Numbers divisible by 2.

2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12

Odd numbers

Numbers not divisible by 2.

1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11

Prime numbers

Exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17… 2 is the only even prime

Composite numbers

Numbers with more than two factors — e.g. 6 has factors 1, 2, 3, 6.

4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12

Factors

A factor is a number that divides another number exactly.

Factors of 12

1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 — 6 factors

Ask students: factors of 18?

1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18

Multiples

Multiples are obtained by multiplying a number by 1, 2, 3, 4…

Multiples of 5

5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30…

Multiples of 7

7, 14, 21, 28, 35…
Factors are usually finite, while multiples continue infinitely. For 12 — factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 (finite). Multiples: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60… (infinite).

Divisibility Rules

Extremely important for aptitude examinations.

RULE · ÷2

Last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8

248 → last digit 8 ✓ divisible by 2

RULE · ÷3

Sum of digits divisible by 3

123 → 1+2+3 = 6 ✓ 6 is divisible by 3

RULE · ÷5

Last digit is 0 or 5

125, 250, 435 ✓ all divisible by 5

RULE · ÷9

Sum of digits divisible by 9

729 → 7+2+9 = 18 ✓ 18 is divisible by 9

RULE · ÷10

Last digit is 0

120, 450, 1000 ✓ all divisible by 10

RULE · ÷11

Alternating digit sums differ by 0 or a multiple of 11

121 → (1+1)−2 = 0 ✓ divisible by 11

HCF — Highest Common Factor

Find the HCF of 12 and 18.

Factors of 12

1, 2, 3, 6, 12

Factors of 18

1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18

Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6 → HCF = 6

LCM — Least Common Multiple

Find the LCM of 4 and 6.

Multiples of 4

4, 8, 12, 16…

Multiples of 6

6, 12, 18…

First common multiple → LCM = 12

The HCF × LCM Formula

For two positive integers, the product of the HCF and LCM equals the product of the numbers themselves.
